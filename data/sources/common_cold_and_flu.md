# Common Cold and Influenza (Flu)

## Overview
The common cold and influenza (flu) are both contagious respiratory illnesses caused by viruses, but they are caused by different viruses and tend to differ in severity. Colds are generally milder, while flu tends to come on more suddenly and hit harder.

## Common Cold: Symptoms and Causes
Colds are usually caused by rhinoviruses, spread through droplets in the air or contact with contaminated surfaces. Typical symptoms include:
- Runny or stuffy nose
- Sneezing
- Sore throat
- Mild cough
- Mild tiredness
- Low or no fever in adults

Symptoms usually build gradually over a day or two and improve within seven to ten days.

## Influenza: Symptoms and Causes
Flu is caused by influenza viruses and tends to appear suddenly, often within hours. Typical symptoms include:
- Sudden high fever
- Body aches and muscle pain
- Chills
- Dry cough
- Fatigue that can be severe, sometimes lasting weeks
- Headache

## Key Differences
- Onset: cold symptoms build gradually; flu symptoms appear suddenly
- Fever: uncommon or mild with a cold; common and higher with flu
- Body aches: mild with a cold; often severe with flu
- Fatigue: mild with a cold; can be pronounced and long-lasting with flu

## Self-Care and General Management
- Rest and adequate sleep
- Staying hydrated with water, herbal tea, or broth
- Using a humidifier or steam inhalation for congestion
- Over-the-counter remedies for symptom relief, used according to package instructions
- Covering coughs and sneezes and washing hands frequently to avoid spreading illness to others
- Staying home while symptomatic, especially in the first few days when contagiousness is highest

## When to See a Doctor
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
- Persistent chest pain or pressure
- Persistent dizziness or confusion
- Symptoms that improve then return with fever and worse cough
- Fever that is very high or lasts more than three to four days
- Underlying conditions such as asthma, heart disease, or a weakened immune system, since these increase the risk of complications

Anyone in a high-risk group (young children, older adults, pregnant women, or people with chronic illness) should have a lower threshold for seeking medical advice.
