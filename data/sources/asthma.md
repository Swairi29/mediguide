# Asthma

## Overview
Asthma is a chronic condition in which the airways in the lungs become inflamed and narrowed, making it harder to breathe. It can vary from mild and occasional to severe and persistent, and symptoms often come in episodes known as asthma attacks.

## Common Causes and Triggers
Asthma often has a genetic and environmental component. Common triggers for symptoms or attacks include:
- Allergens such as pollen, dust mites, pet dander, or mold
- Respiratory infections, such as colds or flu
- Cold air or sudden weather changes
- Physical exercise, especially in cold or dry air
- Air pollution, smoke, or strong fumes
- Stress or strong emotional reactions
- Certain medications in sensitive individuals

## Common Symptoms
- Wheezing (a whistling sound when breathing, especially exhaling)
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness
- Coughing, which may be worse at night or early morning
- Symptoms that worsen with exercise or exposure to triggers

## Self-Care and General Management
- Identifying and avoiding personal triggers where possible
- Using prescribed preventer/controller medication regularly, even when feeling well, since it works over time to reduce inflammation
- Keeping a reliever inhaler accessible at all times for sudden symptoms
- Following a written asthma action plan agreed with a healthcare provider, if one has been provided
- Getting recommended vaccinations, such as annual flu vaccination, since respiratory infections can trigger attacks
- Monitoring symptoms and, if advised, peak flow readings to track control over time

## When to See a Doctor or Seek Emergency Care
- Reliever inhaler is needed more often than usual or stops helping
- Difficulty speaking in full sentences due to breathlessness
- Lips or fingertips turning blue or grey
- Breathing that is visibly labored, with the chest and neck muscles working hard
- Symptoms that do not improve after using a reliever inhaler as directed
- Any sudden, severe breathing difficulty is a medical emergency and needs immediate professional care, not self-management
