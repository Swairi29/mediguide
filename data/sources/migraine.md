# Migraine

## Overview
A migraine is a type of headache disorder that causes moderate to severe throbbing or pulsing pain, usually on one side of the head. It is different from a regular tension headache because it often comes with other symptoms and can last anywhere from a few hours to a few days.

## Common Causes and Triggers
Migraines are not caused by a single thing. Instead, they are usually triggered by a combination of factors, including:
- Hormonal changes, particularly in women around menstruation, pregnancy, or menopause
- Certain foods and drinks, such as aged cheese, processed meats, alcohol (especially red wine), and too much caffeine or caffeine withdrawal
- Stress, whether from work, emotional strain, or major life changes
- Changes in sleep pattern, including too little or too much sleep
- Sensory triggers such as bright or flickering lights, loud noises, or strong smells
- Weather changes, including shifts in barometric pressure
- Skipping meals or dehydration

## Common Symptoms
- Throbbing or pulsing pain, often on one side of the head
- Nausea, sometimes with vomiting
- Sensitivity to light (photophobia) and sound (phonophobia)
- Aura in some people: temporary visual disturbances such as flashing lights, zigzag lines, or blind spots, occurring shortly before or during the headache
- Fatigue and difficulty concentrating

## Self-Care and General Management
- Resting in a quiet, dark room during an attack
- Applying a cold compress to the forehead or neck
- Staying hydrated and keeping regular meal times
- Keeping a headache diary to identify personal triggers
- Maintaining a consistent sleep schedule
- Over-the-counter pain relief may help mild attacks, but should be used according to package instructions and not more often than recommended, since overuse can itself cause rebound headaches

## When to See a Doctor
A person should seek medical attention if:
- Headaches are new, severe, or different from their usual pattern
- The headache is described as "the worst headache of my life"
- It comes with fever, stiff neck, confusion, seizures, double vision, weakness, numbness, or difficulty speaking
- It follows a head injury
- Headaches are getting more frequent or severe over time despite self-care

These "red flag" combinations can indicate a more serious underlying condition and are not things this system is designed to assess — they require urgent professional evaluation.
