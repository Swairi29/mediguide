# Type 2 Diabetes

## Overview
Type 2 diabetes is a long-term condition in which the body either doesn't produce enough insulin or doesn't use insulin effectively, leading to higher-than-normal blood sugar (glucose) levels over time. It is the most common form of diabetes and often develops gradually over years.

## Common Causes and Risk Factors
- Being overweight or having excess fat around the abdomen
- Physical inactivity
- Family history of diabetes
- Age, with risk increasing after 45
- Certain ethnic backgrounds, including South Asian, Black African-Caribbean, and Hispanic populations, which carry higher risk
- History of gestational diabetes
- High blood pressure or abnormal cholesterol levels

## Common Symptoms
- Increased thirst and frequent urination
- Increased hunger
- Fatigue
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing cuts or sores
- Tingling or numbness in hands or feet
- Unintended weight loss in some cases

Symptoms often develop slowly, and some people have no noticeable symptoms for years, which is why routine screening matters for those at higher risk.

## Self-Care and General Management
- Balanced meals with attention to carbohydrate portion sizes and consistent meal timing
- Regular physical activity, such as brisk walking most days of the week
- Monitoring blood glucose levels as advised by a healthcare provider
- Maintaining a healthy weight
- Taking prescribed medication consistently, since stopping or skipping doses without medical advice can cause blood sugar to rise dangerously
- Regular check-ups for eyes, feet, and kidney function, since diabetes can affect these over time

## When to See a Doctor
- Blood sugar readings that are persistently very high or very low, as advised by a care provider
- Symptoms of very high blood sugar: extreme thirst, confusion, rapid breathing, fruity-smelling breath (these can indicate a medical emergency called diabetic ketoacidosis)
- Symptoms of very low blood sugar: shakiness, sweating, confusion, or loss of consciousness
- New or worsening numbness, vision changes, or non-healing wounds, particularly on the feet
- Anyone with symptoms suggesting undiagnosed diabetes (persistent thirst, urination, unexplained weight loss) should be evaluated rather than self-managed
